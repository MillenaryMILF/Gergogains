Delts · Arms · Upper Chest
Three priorities, two protected pillars
  • 1
    Upper chest joins the priority tier. 60° incline pressing and low-to-high cable flys, twice per cycle. The clavicular head is what builds the upper shelf — even on a strong chest it's usually the part that lags.
  • 2
    Shoulders & arms stay priority. Both hit twice per cycle — roughly every 2 days — with the freshest, heaviest work on delts and biceps.
  • 3
    Legs are protected, not neglected. A full, serious session every cycle — squat, RDL, leg press, hamstring and calf work. Frequency is moderate by design, but the volume is real. Never skip it.
  • 4
    Back every 4 days. Once per cycle at maintenance-plus, with the 3-second row eccentric kept in to keep developing it.
  • 5
    Rolling loop. Run Day 1 → 2 → 3 → rest regardless of weekday so the spacing stays true.

Running it well

Upper-chest cues. Keep the 60° bench genuinely steep — most "incline" pressing drifts to 30° and turns into mid-chest. On the cable fly, set the pulleys low and drive hands up and together toward eye level; feel it in the collarbone area, not the front delt.

The rolling cycle. Loop Day 1 → 2 → 3 → Rest regardless of weekday. Upper chest, delts and arms land roughly every 2 days; legs and back every 4. If you ever feel run-down, take the rest day a session early — the loop just resumes.

Don't trade legs away. When time is tight the instinct is to cut the leg day — don't. It's already the lowest-frequency session; skipping it drops legs to once every 8+ days and the balance slips. Cut a set elsewhere instead.

Progression. Double progression: top of the rep range on all sets, then add the smallest jump and rebuild. Log every session.

Cut. ~300–400 kcal under maintenance, protein ≈2g/kg, weights held steady — enough to bring this morning's vascularity into all-day without costing the size you're building up top.

General training guidance for a healthy, experienced lifter — not medical or coaching supervision. Progress loads conservatively, prioritise form over numbers, and back off any movement that causes joint pain rather than muscular fatigue.
