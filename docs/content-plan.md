# Launch content plan

Channels: **TikTok** and **YouTube** (`@gergogains`), plus the Journal on the site.

The whole plan rests on one observation: **there are thousands of fitness accounts
and almost none of them can explain *why* a programme fails.** They can tell you
what to do. You can diagnose. That is the entire differentiator and every piece
of content below is built to demonstrate it rather than assert it.

Do not make transformation content. You are not the biggest person on the app and
you will lose that fight. Make *diagnosis* content, where being an engineer is an
unfair advantage instead of a curiosity.

---

## The one format that matters

### "Send me your programme, I'll find your bottleneck"

Ask people to post their weekly split in the comments. Pick one. Audit it on
camera in 40 seconds: count the sets per muscle, show the table, name the
constraint, give one fix.

This is the single highest-leverage thing you can make, for five reasons:

1. It **is** the $199 product, performed in public. Nobody has to imagine what
   they are buying.
2. It generates its own comments — people post programmes hoping to be picked.
   That is engagement the algorithm rewards, without asking for it.
3. It is infinitely repeatable and never feels repetitive, because the programme
   is different every time.
4. Every episode ends on the same honest CTA: *"want this on yours? the free
   spec sheet does the first half."*
5. It is impossible to fake and impossible to copy from someone who does not
   actually understand programming.

**Run it as a numbered series** — Audit #1, #2, #3. Numbered series are followed;
loose posts are watched once. By #20 the number itself is the proof.

Rules: only use programmes people volunteered, never name or mock anyone, and
never invent a result. The finding is the content — you do not need an outcome.

## Four supporting formats

**1. One principle, one video.** You already have eight written on the site.
Theory of constraints, capacity planning, OEE, spec and tolerance, SPC, root
cause, PDCA, measurement system analysis. Each is a 40-second video: the factory
version, then the training version. Shoot on a whiteboard in black marker — that
matches the site and looks nothing like the rest of the algorithm, which is
mostly gym mirrors and neon.

**2. Programme teardowns.** Take a famous programme — PPL, PHUL, Arnold split,
the one a big YouTuber is selling — and count its actual weekly sets per muscle
against the table. Do it fairly: say what it does well. These pull search traffic
because people query those programmes by name, and being fair to them is what
makes the criticism land.

**3. "Most people get this number wrong."** Volume, frequency, push:pull ratio,
proximity to failure. Each one ends at a calculator that already exists on the
site. This is the only category where you should push a link hard, because the
tool genuinely answers the question the video raises.

**4. The cut, told as measurement.** 97 → 87 kg in eight weeks, 187 logged
sessions. Do not tell it as a transformation story — tell it as *"here is what I
measured, here is where the plan was wrong, here is what I changed."* The
weakness of the before-photo genre is that it proves nothing about method. Your
version proves the method and happens to include a result.

## First 30 days

Batch-record. Do not film daily — film twice, in two sittings, and schedule.

| Week | TikTok (3–4/wk) | YouTube | Journal (site) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Audits #1–3 | Long-form Audit #1 (8–12 min, same programme as TikTok #1, in full) | "How many sets per muscle per week — and why the number in your app is wrong" |
| 2 | Principle 01 + 02, Audit #4 | Shorts cut from week 1 | "Your rear delts are the reason your shoulders look narrow" |
| 3 | Teardown: PPL. Audits #5–6 | Long-form: "I counted every set in the four most popular splits" | "Push-to-pull ratio: the number nobody checks" |
| 4 | "Most people get volume wrong", Principle 03, Audit #7 | Long-form Audit #2 | "What an audit actually looks like" — link `audit-sample.html` |

**Hook discipline.** The first 1.5 seconds carry the whole video. Lead with the
finding, never with a greeting:

- ✅ "This programme has 22 sets of chest and 3 of rear delts."
- ✅ "You are not undertrained. You are 31% wasted."
- ❌ "Hey guys, so today I wanted to talk about..."

**One number on screen at all times.** It is your visual signature and it costs
nothing to maintain.

## What to put in the bio

Send traffic to `gergogains.com` — not straight to checkout. The free spec sheet
is the magnet; it does real work, requires no account, and asking for a $199
audit from a cold viewer converts far worse than asking for nothing.

---

## Two things not to do

**Do not run ads yet.** Paid traffic against an untested landing page burns money
teaching you what 200 organic viewers would have told you for free. Turn ads on
once you know which video makes people build a spec sheet, then buy more of that
exact audience. The GA4 and Google Ads wiring is already in place for when you do.

**Do not promise outcomes.** "Guaranteed shred in 8 weeks" gets pages rejected by
Google Ads and payment processors, and it undercuts the only thing that makes you
different. Your claim is *method*, not result. Keep it that way — it is also the
one claim you can always back up.
